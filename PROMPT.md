# Brief: find the subject for a course-channel

I run a YouTube channel of 6–9 minute animated explainers (2D paper-cutout style: maps, diagrams, step-by-step mechanisms, cutaways, animated systems — no presenter on camera, no live footage, no screen recordings, synthetic narration, no music). I can make about one video a day. The current channel is a grab-bag of curiosity topics. I want to start a SECOND channel that is a complete course: if you watch every video in order, you genuinely learn, become or master something deep, wide and valuable — the way a good university sequence or a great textbook does, not a playlist of trivia.

Your job: explore and recommend what that channel should teach. Be rigorous, cite evidence, and show your reasoning. Write your answer into `ANSWER.md` in this repository (or send it back as one Markdown document in the same structure).

## Step 1 — Candidates

Propose 10 candidate domains broad enough for 150–300 videos forming one coherent curriculum with prerequisites. Examples of the scale I mean: "statistics and probability from counting to inference", "how the human body works, system by system", "economics from the price of bread to central banks", "electronics from electrons to a working radio" — but don't be bound by these; surprise me.

## Step 2 — Score every candidate

Score each candidate 1–5 on every criterion, in one table, with one line of justification per cell:

| Criterion | What it means |
|---|---|
| a) Depth and breadth | Enough for a 150–300 video curriculum with a real mastery ladder (beginner → advanced), not padding |
| b) Retention and binge potential | Do viewers watch in sequence? Is there a next-video pull? Cite retention or series-watching evidence where it exists (named channels, playlists, courses, with numbers and sources) |
| c) Evergreen search demand | Year-round demand with exam- or career-driven peaks; give search-volume or interest evidence with sources |
| d) Fit to my format | Can it be taught well with animated diagrams, maps, cutaways and step-by-step mechanics? Penalise anything that needs hands-on footage, live demos, code typed on screen, or a human face |
| e) Competition and gap | Who already owns this on YouTube (the top 5 channels, their size, their format) and what they do badly or not at all that animation does better |
| f) Fact-checkability | Primary sources exist (textbooks, standards, official data); every claim must be verifiable |
| g) Identity payoff | Can a viewer honestly say "I now understand X" or "I can now do Y"? Is there a course, certificate or product the channel could sell later? |
| h) Audience | Global English audience, with India as a strong second market |

## Step 3 — Your top 3

For each of the three:

- A one-sentence promise ("Watch all of this and you will…").
- The curriculum map: 8–15 modules in order, each with its learning outcome, and the prerequisite graph between them.
- The first 30 video titles in watching order, each with a one-line hook (the question the video answers) and its module.
- The retention design: how each video ends with a reason to watch the next, where the capstone videos sit (the ones that pay off everything before them), and how a newcomer can enter mid-series.
- Packaging: a title grammar for the series, what the thumbnails show, how the playlists are structured.
- The 5 primary sources (books, standards, datasets, syllabi) the whole series should be checked against.
- Risks, and what would make it fail.

## Step 4 — Choose one

Pick ONE and defend the choice against the other two in a paragraph. Then write the first 10 videos as a pilot plan: title, hook, the single mechanism each one teaches, the diagram or animation that carries it, and an estimated difficulty for an animator who draws everything with code.

## Step 5 — Self-critique

What in your analysis is weakly evidenced, what you guessed, and which candidate you nearly chose instead. Name two domains you deliberately rejected and why.

## Rules

- Cite a source with a link for every number; write "estimate" when you are estimating.
- Prefer primary data over blog claims. No filler.
- Format with headings and tables. A thorough report, not a summary.
